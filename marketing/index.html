The Method

Three steps, every month.

See what's counted. Check the books. Make the call. Then keep score on what changed.

1 · See what's counted

Is every sale counted once, and only once?

I start with tracking you can see failing. Tags, the small pieces of code that tell Google or Meta a sale happened, break after a theme update, a checkout change or a new cookie banner. Platforms also take view-through credit: a sale credited to an ad the buyer saw but never clicked. And two platforms can claim the same order. That's default platform counting, not your team's mistake.

2 · Check the books

What was sold and kept, not what was claimed.

Each store order or CRM lead is credited once, to the ad clicked before it, then tied to your month-end close. Orders claimed twice, view-through credit, and sales tax and refunds each come out as a separate step. In the specimen below: $110,000 claimed, $80,000 after an ad click, $65,000 sold and kept.

3 · Make the call

Grow what clears break-even. Hold and test the rest.

Break-even = $1 ÷ your margin. Margin is what $1 of kept sales leaves after product, shipping, payment fees and return costs; finance calls it contribution margin.

50% → 2.0x · 40% → 2.5x · 25% → 4.0x

Clear it on your books and the channel grows, in steps. Miss it and it's tested before any cut, never cut on a dashboard number alone.

Then · Keep score

Did last month's changes happen, and did they pay?

Every change gets an owner, a date and a rule for whether it worked, agreed before the money moves. Next month's verdict checks each one.

Questions

What finance, marketing and owners ask first.

What do I get from the 30-minute call?

Your break-even, worked out from your own margin, and a plain answer on which of your records (orders or CRM leads, ad accounts, books) can tie to your month-end close. If you're in marketing, bring finance: read-only access to the books needs their OK, and you'll both leave with the same break-even. If it's a fit, your first signed verdict covers your latest closed month and arrives within 15 days of read-only access.

Will this be used to cut my budget?

Sometimes, but only after a test, never on a dashboard number alone. It's built to show what has earned more budget too: a channel that clears break-even on your books gets a grow move. View-through credit is left out of the books figure because the books can't confirm it, but that isn't treated as proof it earned nothing, which is why a channel near break-even is held and tested. And whoever runs your ads sees the draft findings, and can answer them with data, before I sign.

Where does break-even come from?

$1 ÷ your margin. Margin is what $1 of kept sales leaves after product, shipping, payment fees and return costs; finance calls it contribution margin. At 40%, each $1 of ads needs $2.50 of kept sales. It isn't gross margin, which leaves out shipping, fees and returns and makes ads look better than they are.

How do you know the ads caused the sales?

Matching shows which sales followed an ad click. It can over- or under-count any platform, so on its own it doesn't prove cause. Only a holdout test (ads off for a test group, compare sales) shows what the ads caused. Where a decision is big enough, the verdict proposes one, with the decision rule fixed in advance and the dates kept clear of your peak season.

Do you replace my agency, or its report?

No. Your agency or team keeps running the campaigns; if it administers the ad accounts, it only needs to add a read-only user. Its report starts from what the platforms record; the verdict starts from your books, what was sold and kept, and checks the platforms against that. Good agencies welcome a scoreboard they didn't write themselves. Nor is this a fractional CMO: a fractional CMO runs your marketing; I tell you what it earned.

What if my tracking is broken?

It usually is, somewhere. That's typically the first finding: which tag is broken, since when, how many sales it's hiding, and the exact fix, written for whoever runs your website or your ad accounts.

What systems do you work with?

Google, Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn or Microsoft Ads; Shopify, WooCommerce or your CRM for leads; QuickBooks, Xero or your accounting team's monthly close. Each gets a read-only role; How it works, step 1 lists the common ones, and I send step-by-step instructions for the rest. If you also sell through Amazon, wholesale or stores, online sales tie to their own revenue line in your books; sales elsewhere aren't credited to ads unless a holdout test measures the lift.

Who sees our books, and what happens to the data?

Me and the software, plus the AI provider that drafts findings from calculated figures (never your logins or ledger). Whoever runs your ads sees the draft findings, not your ledger. Every role is read-only and you can remove it at any time.

Is this an audit?

No. It's management information for your own decisions. It isn't an audit, review or attestation, and it gives no assurance on your financial statements.

What does AI do here, exactly?

One job: it drafts the written findings from figures the software has already calculated. It doesn't do arithmetic, doesn't log into your accounts, and doesn't decide anything. I check every finding and sign the verdict. Your data isn't used to train AI models: the model runs under commercial terms that exclude training on your data.

What does it cost, and how long is the commitment?

A fixed monthly fee, typically $1,000 to $3,000 a month, quoted after the call once I know your channels and systems, and never a share of your ad spend. The first verdict stands on its own. After that it's month to month.
