The Problem

Three systems. Three versions of the truth.

Your website records what visitors did. Each ad platform counts the sales it believes it caused. Your books record what you actually sold and kept. Nobody reconciles the three, so the loudest number wins.

01 · Your website

Was the sale or the lead recorded, once?

A tag is the small piece of code that tells Google or Meta a sale happened. Tags break after a theme update, a checkout change or a new cookie banner. When they do, the platforms bid blind and nobody is told.

Often says: nothing. The tag simply stops.

02 · Your ad platforms

Who gets the credit?

Google, Meta and the rest each count the sales they think they caused, including from people who saw an ad but never clicked it. Two platforms can claim the same customer.

Often says: "$4.60 back for every $1." Spend more.

03 · Your books

Did it make money?

Only the books know your margin, your returns, your fixed costs and your cash. No ad platform sees any of them.

Says: what you actually kept.

Why It Matters

Revenue up $20,000. Profit up $250.

A new paid campaign can lift sales and still leave almost nothing behind once the product is paid for and the ads are paid for. The ad platform reports the $20,000. Only the books show the $250.

Marketing exists to drive revenue, not to drain budgets. If the financials don't show progress, no platform's return figure justifies the spend.

What Every Verdict Answers

Four questions, every month, from the same method.

Tracking

Is every sale and lead recorded, once?

Tags, events and checkout tested against the orders your store or CRM actually took. Breaks are dated, sized and handed to whoever owns the fix.

Truth

What the platforms claim, against what sold.

Sales each platform claims, beside real sales in your books. Orders claimed twice, and credit for people who saw an ad but never clicked, are shown separately.

Profit

What each channel earned after margin.

Sales minus product costs minus ad spend, by channel and product line, against your fixed costs. Not the platform's return figure: dollars you kept.

Execution

What changes next, and did last month's?

Moves priced in dollars, each with an owner, a date and a test of whether it worked, checked again next month.

How It Works

Exactly what happens, step by step.

This is a new kind of service, so here is every step, who or what does it, and what it touches. Nothing in your accounts is ever changed.

  1. 1

    Days 1–3 · You

    Give us view-only access

    About fifteen minutes per account, with step-by-step instructions from us: Google Ads Read only, Meta View performance, GA4 Viewer, a store staff login that sees orders and reports, and your accounting file as Reports only (QuickBooks) or Read only (Xero). None of these roles can change a campaign, a budget or a setting.

  2. 2

    Days 4–7 · Software

    Pull and match the records

    Our software pulls the spend, clicks and claimed sales from each ad platform, every order from your store or CRM, and the closed month from your books. It matches each order to the website visit and the ad click before it, by order ID, and flags orders two platforms both claim and orders no platform claims.

  3. 3

    Days 4–10 · Software

    Calculate every figure

    Profit after ad spend by channel, your break-even return from your own margin, and how many real orders each platform can see. Every figure is saved with its inputs and its formula, so you can ask where any number came from and get the answer. The same code runs every month.

  4. 4

    Days 10–12 · AI, draft only

    Draft the findings

    An AI model reads the calculated figures and last month's record, and drafts the findings: which changes share a cause, what looks broken, which tests are worth running. It doesn't do the arithmetic, doesn't see your logins, and doesn't decide.

  5. 5

    Days 12–14 · Graham

    Check, correct and decide

    Graham checks every finding against the numbers, corrects or removes what doesn't hold, prices each recommended change in dollars, and decides what goes to you. Nothing reaches you unsigned.

  6. 6

    Day 15, then monthly · Graham with you

    The verdict and the meeting

    A short written verdict, and a meeting to walk through it: what's working, what's losing money, what's broken, and the changes for next month, each with an owner and a date. After each month's close, a new verdict scores last month's changes.

Questions

The things owners ask first.

Do you replace my agency?

No. Your agency or team keeps running the campaigns. We measure what they earned against your books and turn that into specific, numbered requests. Good agencies welcome a scoreboard they didn't write themselves.

How is this different from my agency's report?

An agency report starts from what the platforms record. The verdict starts from your books: what you sold, what you kept, and what your fixed costs need. Platform numbers are checked against that, not the other way round.

What if my tracking is broken?

It usually is, somewhere. That's typically the first finding: which tag is broken, since when, how many sales it's hiding, and the exact fix, written for whoever runs your website or your ad accounts.

What access do you need?

View-only roles: Google Ads Read only, Meta View performance, GA4 Viewer, a store login that sees orders and reports, and Reports only (QuickBooks) or Read only (Xero). None of them can change a campaign, a budget or a setting.

Is this a fractional CMO?

No. A fractional CMO runs your marketing. We don't. We tell you, every month, what your marketing actually earned and what to change, and we keep score on whether the changes worked.

Do I need the monthly business review too?

No, the verdict stands on its own. Together they share one set of closed books, so marketing, cash and margin are read from the same numbers.

What does AI do here, exactly?

One job: it drafts the written findings from figures our software has already calculated. It doesn't do arithmetic, doesn't log into your accounts, and doesn't decide anything. Graham checks every finding and signs the verdict.

Is my data used to train AI?

No. Your data is used to analyze your business and for nothing else.

How long is the commitment?

The first verdict stands on its own. After that it's month to month: most of the value compounds, but nothing locks you in.
